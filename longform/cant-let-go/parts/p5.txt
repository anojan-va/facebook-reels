The second thing is the fantasy. When someone leaves, our mind edits the movie. It cuts out the arguments, the times they didn't reply, the way you never quite felt secure. And it keeps the highlights. The best night, the best kiss, the best conversation. So you're not actually missing them. You're missing a trailer your brain put together. And nobody can compete with a trailer. Not the next person you date, and honestly, not even the real version of them.

A friend of mine gave me a weird piece of advice that worked. She said, write down every single time she made you feel small, anxious, or ignored. Every one. I thought it was petty. But when I read that list a week later, I realised I'd spent most of those three months waiting for a text. The relationship I was grieving mostly existed in my head.

The third thing is the most painful one to admit. Sometimes we hold on because letting go means admitting it's really over. As long as you're still thinking about them, there's a tiny part of you that believes it might still happen. The thinking feels like hope. And giving it up feels like losing them a second time.
