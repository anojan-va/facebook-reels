There's an old idea in psychology called the Zeigarnik effect. Back in the 1920s, a psychologist named Bluma Zeigarnik noticed that waiters could remember every detail of an order that hadn't been paid yet. But the moment the bill was settled, the order vanished from their memory. Our minds hold on to things that are unfinished. Open loops. It's the same reason a song you only hear half of gets stuck in your head all day.

And when I read that, I almost laughed. Because that was exactly it. Lena wasn't stuck in my head because she was the love of my life. She was stuck because the story never ended. There was no last conversation. No explanation. No goodbye. My brain was like that waiter, standing there holding an unpaid bill, refusing to let go until someone settled it.

I really believe this is why the "almost" relationships hurt the most. The situationships. The person who disappeared. The one you never got to say everything to. It's not that they mattered more. It's that the ending never happened, so your mind keeps trying to write it. Over and over, every night, a different version. What if I'd said this. What if she'd said that. Your brain isn't remembering. It's trying to finish.
