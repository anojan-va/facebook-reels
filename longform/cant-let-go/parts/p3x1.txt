And here's the second piece. The one that really changed how I saw myself.

The anthropologist Helen Fisher did something fascinating. She put people who had just been rejected in love into a brain scanner, and showed them a photo of the person who left them. And the areas that lit up were the same reward and craving areas you see in addiction. The same system involved in craving a drug.

So that 2 AM feeling? That pull to check their profile one more time? That's not you being weak. That's withdrawal. Your brain got used to a reward, the texts, the attention, the excitement, and now it's craving it like anything else it's hooked on.

When I understood this, I stopped hating myself. I remember sitting on my kitchen floor at midnight, holding my phone, about to send her another "Hey, how have you been?" And for the first time, instead of thinking "I'm so pathetic," I thought, "Oh. This is a craving. It'll pass in ten minutes." And it did. I put the phone down, made some tea, and the wave went past.

That was the first time I felt like I had any control at all.
