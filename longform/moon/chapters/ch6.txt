Chapter six. A world without a Moon.
Think about all the things that would quietly disappear.
No more solar eclipses. The Moon is about four hundred times smaller than the Sun, but also about four hundred times closer, so it fits almost perfectly over it. Without it, the sky's greatest show is gone forever.
No more lunar eclipses. No more blood Moons.
Our calendars would lose their anchor too. The word month comes from Moon, and Ramadan, Easter, Passover, Diwali and Lunar New Year are all timed by its phases.
But some scientists think the Moon's biggest gift came long before any of that.
About four and a half billion years ago, a planet roughly the size of Mars is thought to have smashed into the young Earth. The debris came together to form the Moon.
Back then, the Moon was much closer, and its tides were enormous.
Some researchers think those tides, flooding and drying coastal pools again and again, could have helped the first building blocks of life come together.
Others think the Moon's steadying pull gave life billions of years of fairly stable seasons to evolve.
Nobody knows for sure. But it's possible that without the Moon, we wouldn't be here to miss it.
And here's a myth worth busting. Many people believe the Moon shields us from asteroids. It doesn't. It's far too small and too far away to block more than a tiny fraction of them.
So, what if the Moon disappeared?
The first night would be strangely beautiful, full of stars.
But slowly, the tides would shrink, the animals would lose their clock, and over millions of years, our seasons could start to wobble.
The good news? The Moon isn't going anywhere. It's drifting away by just a few centimetres a year.
In about six hundred million years, it will look too small to cover the Sun, and the last total solar eclipse on Earth will happen.
So next time you see the Moon, take a second look.
It's been holding our world together all along.
