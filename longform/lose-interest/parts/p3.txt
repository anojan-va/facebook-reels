Okay. The second thing, and there's a good chance you're doing it right now. Your phone.

In 2012, two researchers in England, Andrew Przybylski and Netta Weinstein, had pairs of strangers sit down and talk. For some pairs, a phone was simply lying nearby. Not ringing. Not being used. Just there. The pairs with the phone in view felt less close to each other and trusted each other less. Just because a phone was on the table.

I think this is the quietest relationship killer of our time. Every time you glance at your screen in the middle of someone's sentence, you send a tiny message: something else might be more interesting than you. They won't say anything. They'll just stop telling you the good stuff.

A friend once stopped in the middle of a story and said, "Go on, reply to it, I'll wait." She was smiling. But she never finished that story, and I still don't know how it ended.

So put it away. Not face down on the table. Away. In your pocket, in your bag. It's the cheapest way there is to make someone feel important.
