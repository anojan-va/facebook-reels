The third reason is harder to see, because you can be a great listener and still lose people. It happens when you become completely predictable.

The psychologist Arthur Aron has a theory about why we're drawn to certain people. He calls it self-expansion. We like people who make our world bigger, who bring new ideas, new places and new experiences into our lives. And when someone stops doing that, the interest fades, even when nothing is wrong.

Aron and his colleagues tested this with married couples. Some couples spent time each week doing new, exciting activities together. Others did pleasant but familiar things. After ten weeks, the couples who tried new things were more satisfied with their relationship.

I think this is why so many friendships die in the same-place, same-food, same-complaints loop. You meet at the same cafe, tell the same stories about the same people, and you both leave a little bored. Not of each other. Of the routine you've become together.

The fix isn't to become a different person. It's to bring something new to the table. A book you're reading. A strange fact you learned. A plan you've never tried. Ask, "Have you ever wanted to try...?" When new experiences happen with you, people start to connect that feeling of excitement with you.
