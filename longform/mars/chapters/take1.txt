Don't worry. I read them. And I'm going to walk you through every single day of it, the beautiful parts, the terrifying parts, and the part where you find out what a "year" on Mars actually means.

Oh, and a few house rules before we leave. No pets. No pizza delivery. And no complaining. Okay, complaining is allowed. Nobody can hear you anyway.
Day zero. Launch day.
You're strapped into a seat on top of a rocket that is basically a skyscraper full of fuel. Someone counts down from ten. Your heart counts down faster.
Then it feels like an elephant sits on your chest. A friendly elephant. But still an elephant.
Eight minutes later, you're in space. Congratulations again. Now the boring part starts.
Because Mars is not next door. Depending on where the planets are, the trip takes around seven months.
Seven months in a spaceship the size of a small apartment, with the same few people, the same food, and the same smell.
Your water? Recycled. On the space station, almost all of the water, including sweat and urine, gets cleaned and turned back into drinking water.
