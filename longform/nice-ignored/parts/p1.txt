You're the one who remembers everyone's birthday. You're the one who stays late to help, who says yes when you're exhausted, who listens to everybody's problems at two in the morning. You're kind. You're reliable. You never cause drama.

And somehow, you're the one who gets ignored. The selfish guy gets the girl. The loud coworker gets the promotion. The friend who cancels on you every week still has a full group chat, while you sit there wondering why nobody ever thinks of you first.

It feels unfair. It feels like the world rewards bad behaviour and punishes good people. And if you've ever thought, "Maybe I should just stop being nice," stay with me. Because I'm going to tell you why this happens, why it isn't actually about being too nice, and the one change that made people finally start seeing me. It's not what you think, and you definitely don't have to become a jerk.

For most of my twenties, I was the nice guy. Not in a fake way. I genuinely wanted everyone to be happy. If a friend needed a ride to the airport at five in the morning, I was there. If someone at work needed help, I said yes before they even finished the question.

And there was a girl I'll call Chloe. I liked her for almost a year. I listened to her talk about other guys. I helped her move apartments twice. I sent her soup when she was sick. And then she started dating a guy who didn't text her back for days. A guy who forgot her birthday. And she was crazy about him.

I remember sitting in my car after she told me, thinking, "What is wrong with people? What is wrong with me?" I'd done everything right. I'd been the good guy. And I still came second to someone who barely tried.
