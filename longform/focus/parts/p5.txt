The fourth thing is how we rest. Or rather, how we think we rest.

You work for twenty minutes, you feel tired, so you take a break. And what does the break look like? Your phone. Videos, news, messages.

But that's not a break for your brain. That's more input. Faster, louder, more colourful than the work you just left. So when you go back, the work feels even slower and even more boring than before. You've just made the contrast worse.

It's like taking a break from running by sprinting.

And then there's the night. You get into bed, you're exhausted, and you scroll for an hour anyway. Not because it's fun. Because it's the first moment all day that feels like yours, and you don't want to give it up. People call it revenge bedtime procrastination. But you pay for it the next morning, because a tired brain is a distracted brain. Everything in this video gets harder on five hours of sleep.

And there's one more thing that makes all of this worse. Sometimes you can't focus because you don't know what you're supposed to do next.

"Work on the project" isn't a task. It's a fog. And when your brain looks into a fog, it doesn't want to go in. It wants something clear and easy. And what's always clear and easy? The next video.
