So what do you actually do?

The first thing is the easiest, and it works better than any app. Put your phone in another room. Not face down. Not in a drawer next to you. Another room. Remember the study. Distance is the whole point. If you're waiting for something urgent, turn the ringer on and you'll hear it.

The second thing is to train boredom on purpose. Start small. Ten minutes a day where you do nothing. A walk without headphones. Waiting in line without your phone. Sitting with your coffee and just drinking it.

It will feel strange. You'll feel the itch. That itch is the whole point. Every time you sit through it, you're teaching your brain that a slow moment isn't an emergency.

The third thing is to clear the fog before you start. Before you sit down, write the very next physical step. Not "write the report". Something like "open the document and write three bullet points for the introduction". Small enough that your brain can't argue with it.

The fourth thing is to stop trying to focus forever. Pick twenty-five minutes. Just twenty-five. Close every tab except the one you need. And when the time is up, take a real break. Stand up, look out a window, get some water. Anything but a screen.

And if twenty-five minutes feels impossible right now, start with ten. That's fine. You're not trying to become a monk. You're trying to win one small round today, and another one tomorrow.
