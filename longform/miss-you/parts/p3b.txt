And this is where most people get it backwards. They try to make themselves more available, when what they actually need is to make themselves more alive. A life of your own. Your own friends, your own plans, your own weird hobbies, the gym, the guitar you never finish learning. Not to look busy. To actually be someone with a world worth stepping into.

The funniest part is that when I finally started living like this, I wasn't even trying to make anyone miss me. I joined a Thursday night climbing group because a friend dragged me. And three weeks in, the girl I'd just started seeing said, "Wait, you're not free Thursday? What do you even do there?" She was curious. For the first time, someone was curious about my life instead of just expecting me to be in theirs.

There's a principle psychologists talk about called scarcity. Robert Cialdini wrote about it years ago. We value things more when they're a little harder to get. But here's how I see it. Scarcity is ugly when you fake it. It's magnetic when it's real. When you genuinely have a full life, your time becomes valuable on its own. You don't have to pretend.
