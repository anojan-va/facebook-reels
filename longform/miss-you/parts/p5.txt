And there's one more piece to this that changed everything for me. How you leave.

There's a well-known effect in psychology called the Zeigarnik effect. Our minds keep coming back to things that feel unfinished. An interrupted story sticks in your head longer than one that's wrapped up neatly. It's why TV shows end on cliffhangers. And it works with people too, in a much more innocent way.

I used to be the guy who stayed until the very end. The last one at the bar, the last text of the night, the phone call that went on until both of us were yawning. And every conversation slowly ran out of gas. Now I do the opposite. When we're laughing, when the energy is at its best, that's when I say, "I have to go, but tell me the rest of that story tomorrow." And they always do. Because now there's something to come back to.

Leave while it's good. Leave them wanting the next conversation, not relieved that this one is finally over.
