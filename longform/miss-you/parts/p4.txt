The third thing I learned is about the moments themselves. Because when someone misses you, they don't miss you in general. They miss specific moments.

The way you laughed at that terrible movie together. The song you always played in the car. The stupid inside joke that nobody else gets. The way you always order the same dessert and then steal theirs. Nobody has ever said, "I really miss how available he was." They say, "I miss how he made me laugh." "I miss our Sunday pancakes." Moments. Always moments.

I really believe the people we miss most are the ones who leave little pieces of themselves in our everyday lives. So when they hear that song, they think of you. When they walk past that diner, they think of you. That's not manipulation. That's just what happens when you actually share your life with someone.

So stop trying to be impressive and start trying to be memorable. Do something new together. Start a tiny ritual. Give them a story they'll want to tell later. One great evening does more than a hundred "good morning" texts.
