And here's the first thing I learned, and it sounds obvious, but almost nobody lives by it. You cannot miss someone who never leaves. Missing someone needs a gap. A little distance. A moment where you're not there, and they notice.

Now, that does not mean you should disappear on purpose. That's the game, and games are just anxiety wearing a costume. If you go silent to punish someone or to test them, they can feel it. It makes you look insecure, not interesting. I've watched friends do this. They go silent for three days, then check their phone every five minutes to see if the other person noticed. That's not space. That's a hostage situation with your own phone.

The real version is simpler. You stop filling every gap yourself. You let a conversation end without sending one more message. You let them text first sometimes. Not as a tactic. Just because you trust that the connection doesn't need you to hold it up every single second.

After Emma, I made a rule for myself. If I'd sent the last two messages, I'd wait. Not to win anything. Just to see what happened when I stopped carrying the whole conversation. And honestly, the first few times were terrifying. I'd stare at my phone like it owed me money. But something changed. The people who cared started showing up. And the ones who didn't... well, I finally had my answer.
