Trick number six. Nobody is tracking your bad days. Some mornings you wake up and everything feels off. Your hair won't sit right. Your skin looks tired. Your outfit feels wrong. And you're sure everyone will notice that today is a bad day. But in another study, the same Cornell team asked people to predict how much others would notice the ups and downs in their appearance from one day to the next. And once again, people consistently overestimated it. Other people simply don't keep a record of how you looked yesterday. Here's a quick test. Think of three people you saw yesterday. Can you remember what any of them were wearing? Exactly how their hair looked? Probably not. Even close friends rarely notice. Your partner might not even realise you got a haircut. And that's with you paying attention to the world. Now imagine how little they remember about you. Your best days and your worst days blur together into one ordinary picture in other people's minds. So stop treating every bad hair day like a public event. To everyone else, it's just a normal Tuesday. And that brings us to the last trick. It's the strangest one, and it's also the most powerful.
