Trick number five. Assume they liked you more. Think about the last time you met someone new. On the way home, did you replay the conversation? Did you wonder if you talked too much, or said something weird, or if they were just being polite? You're not alone. Almost everyone does this. Your brain replays the tape, looking for every tiny mistake, and it always finds something. In 2018, researchers paired up strangers for a short conversation. Afterwards, they asked each person two questions. How much did you like your partner? And how much do you think your partner liked you? The results were almost the same every time. People liked their partner more than they believed their partner liked them. The researchers called this the liking gap. And it didn't disappear quickly. In one study, it lasted for months between new college roommates. Here's why. While you're busy criticising your own performance, the other person is busy criticising theirs. Neither of you is paying much attention to the other person's little mistakes. You're both standing in your own spotlight. So here's the trick. After any conversation, assume you were liked more than you think. Statistically, you probably were. And the next trick is about something you might worry about every single morning.
