Trick number four. They judge you less harshly than you think. Let's say the worst actually happens. You trip on the stairs in front of everyone. You forget someone's name right after they told you. You spill coffee on your shirt just before a meeting. Surely now, people are judging you. Researchers at Cornell tested this too. They had people fail at a task in front of others, and then predict how harshly those observers would rate them. Again and again, people expected to be judged far more severely than they actually were. Why does this happen? Because when it's you, the blunder is the whole story. You see it in high definition, and you replay it again and again. But the people watching see it as one tiny moment in a much bigger picture. They know you're more than one mistake, because they make mistakes too. In fact, a classic study on the pratfall effect found that when a competent person spilled coffee on themselves, people actually liked them more. The mistake made them seem more human. Perfect people feel distant. Slightly clumsy people feel real. So the next time you mess up, don't spiral. Laugh, fix it, and move on. Because that's exactly what everyone else will do. And trick number five might change how you see every conversation you've ever had.
