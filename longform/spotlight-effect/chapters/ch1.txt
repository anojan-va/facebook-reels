Trick number one. Stop trying to hide your nerves. Picture this. You're about to give a presentation. Your heart is pounding, your hands are sweating, your voice feels shaky, and you're sure everyone in the room can see it. It feels like your anxiety is written all over your face. Psychologists call this the illusion of transparency. In one study at Cornell, people told lies while a group of observers watched and tried to catch them. The liars guessed that about half of the observers would see right through them. In reality, only about a quarter did. Your inner storm is mostly invisible. And here's the part that almost everyone gets wrong. When we feel nervous, we try harder to hide it. We tense up, we avoid eye contact, we rehearse every single word in our head. And that actually makes us look more awkward, not less. So the researchers tried something different. Before a group of nervous people gave a speech, they simply told them about the illusion of transparency. That's it. No breathing exercises. No pep talk. Just the truth: your nerves are far less visible than you think. Those speakers felt calmer, and the audience rated their speeches as better and more composed. So the next time your heart starts racing, remind yourself: they can't see what I feel. Just knowing that switches half the spotlight off. But the next trick goes even further, because it doesn't just calm you down. It makes people like you more.
