Trick number seven. Embarrass yourself on purpose. This sounds crazy, but it's one of the most famous exercises in therapy. The psychologist Albert Ellis created something he called a shame attacking exercise. You deliberately do something mildly embarrassing in public. Nothing harmful, nothing rude, just a little bit odd. Ask a shop assistant what year it is. Wear two different socks. Walk a banana on a lead down the street. Why would anyone do this? Because your brain learns from evidence, not from logic. You can tell yourself a thousand times that nobody cares, but your fear only shrinks when you see it with your own eyes. You do the awkward thing, and the world keeps turning. People glance for a second, and then they go back to their phones. Do it a few times, and the spotlight starts to lose its power over you. So let's recap. Stop hiding your nerves. Point your attention outward. Remember that your mistakes look braver than they feel. You're judged less harshly than you fear. You were probably liked more than you think. Nobody tracks your bad days. And when you're ready, embarrass yourself on purpose. Because here's the truth we started with. You are the centre of your own world. But in everyone else's world, you're a background character. And that's not sad. It's freedom. You can stop performing, and start living.
