Trick number two. Point the spotlight outward. Here's what happens inside an anxious brain at a party, a date, or a job interview. Your attention turns inward. You start watching yourself like a security camera. How do I sound? What are my hands doing? Is my face going red? Did that joke land? Psychologists who study social anxiety call this self-focused attention, and it's the fuel that keeps the spotlight burning. The more you watch yourself, the more awkward you feel. And the more awkward you feel, the more you watch yourself. It's a loop. Imagine two people at the same party. The first one spends the whole night thinking about how they look. The second one spends the night trying to find out one interesting thing about every person they meet. Guess which one goes home feeling awkward? The way out is surprisingly simple. Give your attention a job somewhere else. Get curious about the other person. What are they really saying? What do they care about? What's one question you could ask that nobody else would think of? Your brain can't fully focus on two things at once. Every bit of attention you give to them is attention taken away from your own spotlight. And there's a bonus. Researchers at Harvard found that people who ask more questions, especially follow-up questions, are rated as more likeable. So the trick that calms you down also makes you better company.
