Right now, you probably think people are watching you. Judging your outfit. Replaying that awkward thing you said yesterday. But here's the truth... they're not. Psychologists call this the Spotlight Effect. In the year two thousand, researchers at Cornell University asked students to walk into a room full of strangers wearing a really embarrassing T-shirt. Afterwards, those students guessed that half the room had noticed. The real number? Only about twenty-three percent. Less than one in four. Your brain puts you at the centre of every scene, because you are the centre of your own world. But everyone else is doing the exact same thing. They're too busy worrying about their own spotlight to notice yours. So in this video, I'll show you seven psychological tricks to switch that spotlight off.
