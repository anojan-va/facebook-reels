Chapter two. Impact.
The asteroid reaches the top of the atmosphere, a hundred kilometres up. At twenty kilometres every second, it punches through all of that air in about five seconds.
The air in front of it can't get out of the way. It gets squeezed so hard that it glows hotter than the surface of the Sun.
For anyone looking up, the sky splits open with a blinding white light.
Let's say it comes down exactly where the dinosaur killer did, in a shallow sea off the coast of Mexico.
This rock is so big that when its front edge touches the seabed, its back end is still about as high as a passenger jet flies.
To an object this size, the ocean is barely a puddle.
In less than a second, it releases about a hundred million megatons of energy. That's billions of times the bomb that destroyed Hiroshima, and more than ten thousand times every nuclear weapon on Earth put together.
