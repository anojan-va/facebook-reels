The plants came back too. In rock layers just above the impact, fossil pollen suddenly gives way to fern spores. Ferns were the first plants to cover the burned land.
In a strange way, we're only here because an asteroid cleared the stage.
But this time, the giants are us.
We're big, we need a lot of food, and our whole civilisation depends on a stable climate. A world after impact would have no global trade, no internet, and no supermarkets.
What it would have is what we've saved. Libraries. Survivors with skills. And places like the Global Seed Vault in Norway, buried inside a frozen mountain, holding more than a million samples of the world's crops.
The world it leaves behind is colder, darker and emptier, and then, over thousands of years, green again.
Which brings us back to the question from the very beginning.
