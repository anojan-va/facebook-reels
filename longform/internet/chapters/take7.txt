Hundreds of cables, some as thin as a garden hose, carry almost all the data that moves between continents.
And they break all the time. Around one hundred and fifty to two hundred times a year. Mostly because of ship anchors and fishing nets.
And yes, sharks have bitten them too. Nobody knows why. Maybe they just wanted Wi-Fi.
Taking the whole internet down at once is incredibly hard. It was designed to route around damage.
But smaller blackouts have happened.
In twenty eleven, Egypt switched off its internet for about five days.
And in twenty twenty one, Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp vanished for about six hours, because of one bad configuration change. Some engineers reportedly struggled to get into their own buildings, because the door badges needed the network too.
Imagine being locked out of the building, while trying to fix the thing that locked you out.
And fun fact. The very first internet message, in nineteen sixty nine, was supposed to be the word login.
The system crashed after two letters. So the first message ever sent on the internet was just, L O.
The internet has been crashing since day one. Today, it just has a lot more people watching.
