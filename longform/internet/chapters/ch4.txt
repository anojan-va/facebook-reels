Day three.
You want to visit your friend across town. Easy. You open the map app.
And here's a fun fact. Your phone still knows exactly where you are. GPS comes from satellites, not the internet.
So you get a little blue dot, in the middle of a completely blank screen. You know where you are. You just don't know where anything else is.
So you do something wild. You buy a paper map. At a petrol station. With your one coin.
It takes twenty minutes to unfold and the rest of your life to fold back up.
Day four.
Your phone is now the world's most expensive flashlight.
And slowly, something strange happens.
People start talking to each other. On purpose. In real life.
Your neighbours, who you've only ever nodded at, turn out to have names. One of them has a radio. Suddenly, she's the most popular person on the street.
At the library, there's a queue. A queue! For books! The librarian looks like she's waited her whole life for this moment.
Families drag out board games nobody has played in years. Someone flips the board. The game is over. Some traditions never change.
Kids go outside. Like, outside outside. They invent a game with a ball and a stick, and argue about the rules for three hours.
And dating? Dating apps are gone. If you like someone, you have to walk up to them and say hello. With your actual mouth. Terrifying.
Meanwhile, hospitals switch to their backup plans. Paper charts, pens, and runners carrying notes down the hall. Slower, but they keep working.
Because hospitals plan for days exactly like this.
That night, you lie in bed and stare at the ceiling. No scrolling. No last quick video. Just you and your thoughts.
It turns out your thoughts are kind of loud. Who knew?
