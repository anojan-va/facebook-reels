Day six.
Something has changed. And it's you.
You slept eight hours. Nobody kept you up scrolling at two in the morning.
You read half a book. You learned your neighbour's dog's name. It's Biscuit. Biscuit is a good boy.
You still reach for your phone sometimes. But now, you notice yourself doing it.
Your brain feels quieter. Like someone finally turned down a radio you didn't know was playing.
And here's the funny thing. You don't know what's trending. You don't know who's angry about what. And the world still turned.
Day seven.
At three in the afternoon, your phone buzzes. And this time, it's real.
The internet is back.
And then it buzzes again. And again. And again.
Every message, every email, every notification from the last seven days arrives at once. Your phone gets so hot you could fry an egg on it.
Remember, the world normally sends hundreds of billions of emails every day. And everyone, everywhere, is trying to log back in at the same moment.
So the internet comes back, and immediately falls over again. Classic internet.
Your boss emails. Subject line: urgent, sorry for the delay.
Your apps all need updates. Your group chat has nine hundred unread messages, and every one of them says, is your internet down too?
Your photos start uploading. All of them. Including the forty blurry pictures you took of your neighbour's radio.
You open your favourite app, and for a second, it feels amazing. The noise rushes back in, like walking out of a quiet room into a stadium.
Then you look up, and notice something. The street is quiet again. Everyone is looking down at their phones. Just like before.
And this is the hardest part. Not losing the internet. Getting it back, and deciding how much of your life to give it.
So, the internet is back on. You're holding your phone. Biscuit is waiting at the door.
What do you do first?
