Some experts think many steel bridges would collapse within a few centuries. Big suspension bridges might fall even sooner, once their cables corrode.
Inside our tall buildings, the steel bars hidden in the concrete are rusting too. Rust takes up more space than steel, so it pushes the concrete apart from the inside. Bit by bit, the towers start to crumble.
Most wooden houses are gone. Our suburbs look like forests with a few brick chimneys poking out.
Animals have moved in everywhere. Deer graze where shopping centres used to be. Bears and wolves return to places they haven't seen in centuries.
Elephants in Africa, no longer hunted, slowly spread back across their old lands. Fish return to rivers. Coral reefs, without our pollution and fishing boats, begin to recover in many places.
But some of what we left behind won't go away so easily.
Plastic doesn't really rot. It breaks into smaller and smaller pieces that can last for centuries. Our bottles, bags and toys are now a permanent part of the ocean.
And the carbon dioxide we put into the air won't disappear quickly either. Most of it will be absorbed by the oceans over centuries, but some of it will stay in the atmosphere for thousands of years.
