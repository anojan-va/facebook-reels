Plus one day.
One by one, the lights go out. Cities that glowed for a hundred years go dark.
Some places last a little longer. Solar panels keep humming in the sun. Hydroelectric dams can run on their own for weeks or even months, until something clogs or breaks.
But from space, the night side of Earth looks the way it did before Thomas Edison.
And deep under New York City, something is starting to happen.
Every day, pumps under New York pull millions of litres of groundwater out of the subway tunnels. Without electricity, those pumps stop.
Some engineers estimate that within a day or two, parts of the subway would start to flood.
The city's foundations are quietly filling with water, and nobody is there to notice.
Plus one week.
Now the real danger begins. There are around four hundred nuclear reactors on Earth.
Most of them are built to shut down automatically. But even a sleeping reactor needs cooling, and so does its used fuel.
Backup generators keep the water flowing for a while. Then the diesel runs out.
